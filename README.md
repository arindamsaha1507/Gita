# गीतासूत्रजालम्
## श्लोकतः धातुपर्यन्तम्

> अस्माकं जालकेन्द्रे स्वागतम्।

संस्कृतभाषायां कोटिशः साधुशब्दाः प्रायेण द्विसहस्रेभ्यो धातुभ्यो व्युत्पन्नाः इति बहुप्रचलितं कथनं संस्कृतवैयाकरणानां मध्ये। उदाहरणार्थं **रामः पठति** इति वाक्यं यदि स्वीकुर्मः तर्हि **रामः** इति पदं **रमुँ** धातोः जातं तथा च **पठति** इति पदं **पठ्** धातोः जातम्। इदानीं द्वौ प्रश्नौ मनसि सहजेन एव उत्पद्येते।

के ते द्विसहस्राः धातवः। सर्वेषां धातूनाम् आवलिः धातुपाठः इति ज्ञायते।

पदानां धातूनां च मध्ये कः सम्बन्धः। पदानि कुतो व्युत्पन्नानि इति कथं ज्ञातव्यम्। तदर्थं व्याकरणशास्त्रस्य अष्टाध्यायी इति मूलग्रन्थस्य सूत्राणि प्रयोक्तव्यानि।

**रामः पठति** इत्युदाहरणं तु सरलं वाक्यम्। किन्तु लोके शास्त्रे व्यवहारे च अयं सिद्धान्तः कथं सिध्यति। के धातवः न्यूनप्रयुक्ताः। के च बहुप्रयुक्ताः। एतेषां प्रश्नानाम् उत्तराणाम् अन्वेषणम् अस्माकं लक्ष्यम्।

अस्मिन् प्रकल्पे वयं **श्रीमद्भगवद्गीतायां प्रयुक्तानि** सूत्राणि धातूंश्च ज्ञातुं प्रवृत्ताः स्मः। इदं कार्यं पञ्चसु सोपानेषु प्रकल्पितम्।

1.  **सन्धिविच्छेदः** पदानां मध्ये यत् सन्धिकार्यं वर्तते तद् विभज्य ससूत्रं पदानां पृथक्करणम्। यथा अत्र पाण्डवाः च एव इति पदानां पृथक्करणम्।
2.  **सुबन्तरूपसिद्धिः** सुबन्तानां व्युत्पत्तिप्रक्रियायाः ससूत्रं लेखनम्। यथा युयुत्सवः इति युयुत्सु प्रातिपदिकस्य प्रथमाबहुवचनान्तरूपम्।
3.  **तिङन्तरूपसिद्धिः** क्रियापदानां व्युत्पत्तेः ससूत्रं प्रतिपादनम्। यथा डुकृञ् + झि = अकुर्वन्।
4.  **समासविग्रहः** समासानाम् अर्थानां ससूत्रं स्थापनम्। यथा धर्मस्य क्षेत्रम् इति धर्मक्षेत्रम्।
5.  **प्रातिपदिकव्युत्पत्तिः** प्रातिपदिकानां धातुमूलपर्यन्तं विग्रहस्य संस्थापनम्। यथा धृ + मन् = धर्म क्षि + ष्ट्रन् = क्षेत्र।

सम्प्रति वयं प्रथमे सोपाने वर्तामहे। गीतायाः आदिमानां त्रयाणाम् अध्यायानां श्लोकेषु प्रयुक्तानि पदानि पृथक् कृत्वा वामभागे श्लोकसञ्चये दत्तानि। तत्र प्रत्येकं श्लोके पदानां मध्ये यानि सन्धिकार्याणि यैः सूत्रैः सिध्यन्ति तानि सूत्राण्यपि प्रदर्शितानि। इत्थम् आहत्य श्लोकानां सूत्राणां पदानां च मध्ये यत् जालकर्म निर्मितं तद् अधोदत्तम्।

> यदि अस्मिन् प्रकल्पे साहाय्यं कर्तुं रुचिः अस्ति तर्हि श्लोकपृष्ठानि गत्वा संशोधनानि प्रेषयत॥

# The Gita's Web of Rules
## From Verse to its Root Word

> Welcome to our project!

Among Sanskrit grammarians, there is a very common saying: "In the Sanskrit language, millions of valid words are derived from about two thousand verbal roots."

To understand this, let's take a simple sentence as an example: **रामः पठति** (Rāmaḥ paṭhati), which means "Rama reads."

In this sentence, the word **रामः** (Rāmaḥ) is derived from the root **रमुँ** (ramuँ - *to delight in*), and the word **पठति** (paṭhati) is derived from the root **पठँ** (paṭhaँ - *to read*).

This naturally brings two questions to mind:

1.  What are these two thousand roots? The complete list of all these roots is called the **Dhatupatha** (धातुपाठ).
2.  What is the relationship between finished words and their roots? How can we know where a word comes from? To figure this out, we must use the rules (*sutras*) from the foundational text of Sanskrit grammar, the **Ashtadhyayi** (अष्टाध्यायी).

### Explaining the Hard Concepts

*   **Verbal Root (धातु - Dhatu):** Think of a root as the most basic, core part of a word that holds its essential meaning. It's like the root `port` in English, which gives us words like *im**port**, ex**port**, trans**port**, and **port**able*. All these words have a core meaning related to "carrying." In Sanskrit, a *dhatu* is that fundamental seed of meaning.

*   **Ashtadhyayi (अष्टाध्यायी):** This is the masterpiece of the ancient linguist Pāṇini. It's a comprehensive grammar of Sanskrit laid out in about 4,000 extremely short, code-like rules called **sutras** (सूत्राणि). These *sutras* act like mathematical formulas or programming logic that transform a root into a complete, usable word by adding prefixes, suffixes, and making other changes.

The example "Rama reads" is very simple. But how does this principle work in the real world of scriptures, literature, and everyday use? Which roots are used frequently, and which are rare? Our goal is to find the answers to these questions.

In this project, we are focused on identifying the **grammatical rules (sutras) and verbal roots (dhatus) used in the Śrīmad Bhagavad Gītā**.

This work is planned in five steps:

---

### The Five Steps of Analysis

1.  **Sandhi-viccheda (सन्धि-विच्छेदः) - Splitting Conjoined Words**
    *   **Explanation:** In Sanskrit, when two words are spoken one after another, the sounds at their boundaries often blend together to create a smooth flow. This is called *Sandhi*. This step involves separating these blended words back into their original forms and identifying the specific grammar rule (*sutra*) that caused the blending.
    *   **Example:** In the Gita, the phrase `पाण्डवाश्चैव` (pāṇḍavāścaiva) is a result of *sandhi*. This step breaks it down into its three original words: `पाण्डवाः` (pāṇḍavāḥ) + `च` (ca) + `एव` (eva).

2.  **Subantānām rūpasiddhiḥ (सुबन्तानाम् रूपसिद्धिः) - Derivation of Nouns & Adjectives**
    *   **Explanation:** This step shows the complete process of how a base noun or adjective (called a *prātipadika*) gets its final form, including its case (e.g., subject, object) and number (singular, plural). It's like showing how "person" becomes "person's" or "people."
    *   **Example:** The word `युयुत्सवः` (yuyutsavaḥ - *those wishing to fight*) is the plural subject form derived from the base word `युयुत्सु` (yuyutsu - *one who wishes to fight*).

3.  **Tiṅantarūpasiddhiḥ (तिङन्तरूपसिद्धिः) - Derivation of Verbs**
    *   **Explanation:** This is the process of building a finished verb. It shows how a verbal root is combined with prefixes, suffixes, and other elements to give it a specific tense (past, present, future), person (I, you, he/she), and number (singular, plural).
    *   **Example:** The final verb `अकुर्वत` (akurvata - *they did*) is derived from the root `डुकृञ्` (ḍukṛñ - *a root for 'to do'*) and the suffix `झ` (jha), which indicates the third-person plural past tense.

4.  **Samāsa vigrahaḥ (समास विग्रहः) - Analysis of Compound Words**
    *   **Explanation:** Sanskrit is famous for its long compound words, where multiple nouns and adjectives are joined into one. This step involves breaking down a compound word and explaining its meaning by showing the relationship between its parts.
    *   **Example:** The word `धर्मक्षेत्रे` (dharmakṣetre - *on the field of dharma*) is a compound. Its analysis is `धर्मस्य क्षेत्रम्` (dharmasya kṣetram), which means "the field *of* righteousness/duty."

5.  **Prātipadikānām vyutpattiḥ (प्रातिपदिकानाम् व्युत्पत्तिः) - Derivation of Base Words**
    *   **Explanation:** This is the deepest level of analysis. It traces a base word (the *prātipadika* from Step 2) all the way back to its most fundamental verbal root and the suffix that created it.
    *   **Example:**
        *   The word **धर्म** (dharma - *righteousness, duty*) is derived from the root **धृ** (dhṛ - *to hold, to sustain*) + the suffix **मन्** (man).
        *   The word **क्षेत्र** (kṣetra - *field*) is derived from the root **क्षि** (kṣi - *to dwell*) + the suffix **ष्ट्रन्** (ṣṭran).
